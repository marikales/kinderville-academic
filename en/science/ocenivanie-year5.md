# Assessment · Year 5 · Science

*Based on the thematic planning by Daria Magomedova.*

## The four levels

The same ones as in the school documents and in reports to parents.

- **Below — not there yet.** Needs individual support.
- **Working Towards — on the way to the standard.** Does it partly, with mistakes, or with direct help.
- **Meeting — the expected standard for the year.** The child confidently does what was covered in lessons.
- **Exceeding — beyond the standard.** Applies it in a new situation: works through an unfamiliar example, transfers the conclusion to another case, predicts before the experiment.

**The level is given per topic, not per child.** The same child can be Exceeding in one topic and Working Towards in the next. That's normal, and it says nothing about the child overall.

Thinking and Working Scientifically (observing, measuring, recording, testing a prediction) is assessed separately — [one shared page for all classes](en/science/ocenivanie-issledovanie.md). The English science lesson is assessed against that page too.

## Earth and Space

| Topic | Below | Working Towards | Meeting — the expected standard | Exceeding |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. The Earth's movement** (6.1) | Can't explain why the seasons change | Explains the phenomena; gets muddled in the terms | Explains the Earth's rotation on its axis and its orbit around the Sun; links them to day, night and the year | Explains an unfamiliar case: why summer comes at different times in the two hemispheres |
| **2. Seasonal changes** (6.2) | Doesn't connect the tilt with the seasons | Describes the seasonal changes; names the cause imprecisely | Explains that the seasons come from the tilt of the axis, not from the distance to the Sun | Works out why there are almost no seasons at the equator, and polar day at the poles |
| **3. The role of gravity** (5.1) | Can't explain how the pull acts | Understands the basics; explains with mistakes | Explains how gravity holds objects on the Earth and bodies in space | Explains an unfamiliar situation: weightlessness on a space station, weight on another planet |
| **4. Satellites and their movement** (5.2) | Can't explain how satellites move | Gets the idea; struggles with the details | Explains why a satellite doesn't fall to Earth, and how a natural satellite differs from an artificial one | Gives examples of what satellites are used for and explains why an orbit is exactly what's needed for it |
| **5. The Earth's atmosphere and water** | Has no picture of the atmosphere | Knows roughly; struggles to name the composition | Knows there's a layer of air around the Earth made of a mixture of gases; understands that almost all water on Earth isn't pure, but has substances dissolved in it | Explains where the dissolved substances in drinking water come from and how they're removed |
| **6. Pollution** | Doesn't tell pollution from natural litter | Names examples; only partly explains the cause and the harm | Explains that pollution is what humans put in and what harms nature; gives local examples | Suggests how to check or reduce pollution in a specific place near the school |
| **7. The water cycle** | Can't describe the cycle | Names the stages; muddles the order or the causes | Describes the cycle: evaporation, condensation, precipitation; shows it on a diagram | Uses the cycle to explain a local phenomenon: the rainy season, morning dew, mist over the sea |
