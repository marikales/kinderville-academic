# Assessment · Year 4 · Science

*Based on the thematic planning by Daria Magomedova.*

## The four levels

The same ones as in the school documents and in reports to parents.

- **Below — not there yet.** Needs individual support.
- **Working Towards — on the way to the standard.** Does it partly, with mistakes, or with direct help.
- **Meeting — the expected standard for the year.** The child confidently does what was covered in lessons.
- **Exceeding — beyond the standard.** Applies it in a new situation: works through an unfamiliar example, transfers the conclusion to another case, predicts before the experiment.

**The level is given per topic, not per child.** The same child can be Exceeding in one topic and Working Towards in the next. That's normal, and it says nothing about the child overall.

Thinking and Working Scientifically (observing, measuring, recording, testing a prediction) is assessed separately — [one shared page for all classes](en/science/ocenivanie-issledovanie.md). The English science lesson is assessed against that page too.

## Earth and Space

| Topic | Below | Working Towards | Meeting — the expected standard | Exceeding |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. What the Earth is made of** (4.1) | Can't name the layers, or muddles their order | Lists the layers but can't describe their properties | Names the core, mantle and crust, and describes each layer | Explains how people know about the layers when no one can get down there |
| **2. Earthquakes, volcanoes, landforms** (4.2–4.3) | Confuses the phenomena, can't explain where they come from | Knows the phenomena but doesn't link them to their causes | Explains why earthquakes and eruptions happen, and links them to the movement of the crust | Works through an unfamiliar case: why volcanoes come in chains, why some places get earthquakes and others don't |
| **3. Day and night** (5.5) | Doesn't understand why day and night change | Explains partly, with mistakes | Explains day and night by the Earth's rotation; shows it on a model with a light source | Explains why day comes at different times in different countries |
| **4. The Moon and its phases** (6.2–6.3) | Doesn't understand why the Moon changes its appearance | Knows the main phases, muddles the order | Names the cycle of phases and explains why the Moon's appearance changes | From a few days of observation, predicts what the Moon will look like next |
| **5. Gravity and motion** (5.2) | Doesn't understand how the pull works | Understands partly; can't give examples of their own | Explains how gravity acts on objects on Earth and keeps the Moon and satellites in place | Explains an unfamiliar example: why you can jump higher on the Moon, why a satellite doesn't fall |
| **6. The planets of the Solar System** | Has no picture of how the system is built | Names the planets, muddles the order | Names the planets in order and knows the Sun is at the centre | From data about a planet (size, distance), works out which one it is |

| **7. Energy around us** (2.1) | Doesn't tell types of energy apart | Knows a few types; makes mistakes classifying | Tells types of energy apart and gives examples of where each one is at work | Finds the energy in an unfamiliar device and names which kind it is and where it comes from |

|---|---|---|---|---|
| **12. ## Electricity and Magnets
