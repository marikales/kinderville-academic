# Assessment · Year 6 · Science

*Based on the thematic planning by Daria Magomedova.*

## The four levels

The same ones as in the school documents and in reports to parents.

- **Below — not there yet.** Needs individual support.
- **Working Towards — on the way to the standard.** Does it partly, with mistakes, or with direct help.
- **Meeting — the expected standard for the year.** The child confidently does what was covered in lessons.
- **Exceeding — beyond the standard.** Applies it in a new situation: works through an unfamiliar example, transfers the conclusion to another case, predicts before the experiment.

**The level is given per topic, not per child.** The same child can be Exceeding in one topic and Working Towards in the next. That's normal, and it says nothing about the child overall.

Year 6 is the final year of primary school, so the expected standard here leans more on explaining through causes: not just "what happens", but "why exactly" and "how we know".

Thinking and Working Scientifically (observing, measuring, recording, testing a prediction) is assessed separately — [one shared page for all classes](en/science/ocenivanie-issledovanie.md). The English science lesson is assessed against that page too.

## Earth and Space

| Topic | Below | Working Towards | Meeting — the expected standard | Exceeding |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. The Solar System** (6.3) | Doesn't describe how the system is built; doesn't understand the role of gravity | Names the planets and knows the order; explains movement and gravity imprecisely | Describes the arrangement and movement of the planets, the Moon and the Sun; explains what keeps the planets in their orbits | From data about an unfamiliar body (distance, size, orbital period), draws a reasoned conclusion about what it is |
| **2. The Moon's monthly cycle** | Can't describe the cycle | Names the phases; explains the Moon's appearance imprecisely | Describes how the Moon's appearance changes over a month, and explains why | From a few days of observations, works out which phase the Moon is in and what comes next |
| **3. Igneous rocks** (3.1) | Doesn't identify the rock type | Knows where they come from; struggles to name the features | Explains that igneous rocks form when magma cools; recognises them by their features | From the look of an unfamiliar sample, makes a reasoned guess about how it formed |
| **4. Sedimentary rocks and fossils** (3.2) | Doesn't explain how sedimentary rocks form | Describes the process partly; explains the role of the layers imprecisely | Explains how layers of sediment turn into rock and how fossils are preserved in it | From the layers in a picture, works out what happened earlier and what happened later |
| **5. Metamorphic rocks and the rock cycle** (3.3) | Can't describe the cycle; muddles the rock types | Tells the three types apart; describes the cycle partly | Explains how heat and pressure change rock; describes the cycle: hardening, weathering, build-up, burial, remelting | Traces a particular stone around the cycle and explains what it once was and what it will become |
| **6. Soil** (3.4) | Doesn't tell soil from plain dirt; can't name what's in it | Names the components; struggles to tell types apart | Knows that soils differ, and tells them apart by their content of sand, clay and humus | From a description of a soil, works out what will grow well in it and what won't, and justifies it |

## Forces

| Topic | Below | Working Towards | Meeting — the expected standard | Exceeding |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **9. Mass and weight** (5.1) | Treats mass and weight as the same thing | Tells the words apart; explains the difference imprecisely | Tells mass from weight, knows the units (kilograms and newtons); explains that when gravity changes, weight changes but mass doesn't | Calculates, or makes a reasoned estimate of, an object's weight on the Moon, and explains why the mass stays the same |
| **10. Forces and motion** (5.2) | Doesn't build a force diagram | Draws the arrows; muddles the size or the direction | Shows with arrows the name, direction and size of the forces on an object — both at rest and in motion | Works through an unfamiliar situation with arrows and explains why the motion is the way it is |
| **11. Floating and density** (5.3) | Can't explain why things float or sink | Knows about mass; leaves out the role of shape | Explains what decides whether an object sinks or floats, taking both mass and shape into account | Explains how to make both a sinking and a floating object from the same material, and tests it |

## Substances

| Topic | Below | Working Towards | Meeting — the expected standard | Exceeding |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **13. Properties of substances** (2.1) | Lists properties at random | Names properties; struggles to apply them to an unfamiliar substance | Describes a substance by its properties: hardness, solubility, transparency, conductivity; knows that melting and boiling points are properties too | Identifies an unfamiliar substance from a set of measured properties, and justifies the conclusion |
| **14. Thermal and electrical conductivity** (2.2) | Doesn't understand conductivity as a property | Names conductors; mixes up thermal and electrical conductivity | Explains that conductivity is a property of a substance; tells good conductors from poor ones by experiment | Chooses a material for a task (to heat, to keep warm, to insulate) and justifies the choice |
| **15. Reversible and irreversible changes** (2.3) | Doesn't tell the types of change apart | Tells the examples apart; struggles to justify | Tells a reversible change from an irreversible one; explains how temperature affects dissolving | From an unfamiliar experiment, works out the type of change and suggests how to test whether it's reversible |
| **16. Chemical reactions** (2.4) | Doesn't tell a chemical change from a physical one | Names the signs; struggles to apply them to a new case | Knows that in a reaction the starting substances turn into new ones; names the signs: a gas, a colour change, a temperature change | From an unfamiliar experiment, decides whether there was a reaction, and argues from the signs |

## Living Things

| **21.
