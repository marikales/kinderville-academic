# Assessment · Year 3 · Science

*Based on the thematic planning by Daria Magomedova.*

## The four levels

The same ones as in the school documents and in reports to parents.

- **Below — not there yet.** Needs individual support.
- **Working Towards — on the way to the standard.** Does it partly, with mistakes, or with direct help.
- **Meeting — the expected standard for the year.** The child confidently does what was covered in lessons.
- **Exceeding — beyond the standard.** Applies it in a new situation: works through an unfamiliar example, transfers the conclusion to another case, predicts before the experiment.

**The level is given per topic, not per child.** The same child can be Exceeding in one topic and Working Towards in the next. That's normal, and it says nothing about the child overall.

**This page is about the Russian lesson.** Here we look for understanding of the topic: the cause, "why I think so", weighing up competing ideas. The bar here is the same for everyone and does not depend on language — see the [Course aims](en/science/README.md).

**The English lesson is assessed differently** — against the [Thinking and Working Scientifically page](en/science/ocenivanie-issledovanie.md): same topic, but the bar is personal, set by the child's level of English.

## Topics of the year

| Topic | Below | Working Towards | Meeting — the expected standard | Exceeding |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. The shape of the Earth, Sun and Moon** (6.1) | Doesn't tell the Earth, Sun and Moon apart by their roles; names their shape only with a prompt | Names the bodies and their shape, but gets confused about what moves around what | Knows the Earth, Sun and Moon are spheres; shows on a model how they are arranged and how they move relative to each other | Works through an unfamiliar case on the model: why the Sun and the Moon look almost the same size from Earth |
| **2. The Moon and its phases** (6.2, 6.3) | Doesn't connect how the Moon looks with how it moves | Recognises the phases but muddles the order, or thinks the Moon itself changes shape | Names the phases in order; explains that it's not the Moon that changes, but the lit part of it that we can see | From a photo of an unfamiliar phase, works out which one it is and what comes next |
| **3. Gravity** (5.2) | Explains falling by other causes ("heavy things just fall") | Knows the word and the general idea; gives examples only with a prompt | Explains that objects fall because the Earth pulls them towards itself; gives examples of their own | Transfers it to a new case: why the pull keeps the Moon near the Earth, and what would happen without it |
| **4. Solids, liquids and gases** (2.1) | Sorts at random, can't name the features | Tells solid from liquid; gas is hard; names the features imprecisely | Sorts familiar substances into the three states and names the key feature of each: keeps its shape / flows / fills all the space | Works out the state of an unfamiliar substance and justifies it by a feature; takes on a tricky case (sand, foam, flour) |
| **5. How shadows change** (3.1) | Doesn't connect a shadow with a light source | Can make a shadow, but explains the changes by guessing | Explains that a shadow appears when an object blocks the light; shows how to change its size by moving the source or the object | Predicts what the shadow will be like when the light moves to a new position, and tests it |
| **6. Transparent and translucent materials** (3.2–3.4) | Doesn't tell the groups apart, doesn't use the words | Tells transparent from opaque; muddles translucent | Sorts materials into three groups by how light passes through them; uses the words transparent, translucent, opaque | Picks a material for a purpose and explains the choice: what to use for a window, what for a curtain, what for a screen |
| **7. Living or non-living** (1.1) | Relies on movement as the only sign of life | Tells the obvious cases apart; doesn't list all the signs | Tells living from non-living by a set of signs: it feeds, breathes, grows, reproduces, moves | Takes on a hard case — a seed, a dry branch, fire, a car — and argues from the signs |
| **8. Parts of plants and their jobs** (1.2) | Recognises the parts only with a prompt | Names the parts, muddles their jobs | Names root, stem, leaf and flower, and says what each part does | On an unfamiliar plant, finds the parts and explains why they're built the way they are |
| **9. Light, water and warmth in the life of plants** (1.3, 1.4) | Lists the conditions only with a prompt | Names the conditions; only partly explains the link with growth | Explains what a plant needs to live, and predicts what will happen if one condition is taken away | Comes up with a way to test one condition while keeping the others the same; explains the result of someone else's experiment |
| **10. Animal groups, life cycles and food chains** (4.2, 4.3) | Can't build a chain, muddles the groups | Tells some groups apart; builds a chain only with help | Puts an animal into a group by its features; builds a simple food chain and says which part is the plant and who feeds on it | Builds a chain for an unfamiliar place; explains what will happen if one link is removed |
| **11. Fossils** (4.4) | Confuses a fossil with an ordinary stone | Knows fossils are the remains of ancient creatures, but can't explain what they're useful for | Explains that a fossil is the trace of a creature that lived long ago, and that it can tell us about the past | From a picture of an unfamiliar fossil, makes a reasoned guess about what it was and where it lived |
| **12. Separating mixtures** (2.2–2.4) | Doesn't choose a method, acts at random | Can follow a method from instructions, struggles to choose one | Chooses a method to suit the mixture — picking out, sieving, filtering — and does it hands-on | Invents a method for an unfamiliar mixture and explains which property it relies on |
| **13. Friction and forces** (5.1, 5.3) | Doesn't connect stopping with the surface | Knows the word; explains the effect of the surface imprecisely | Explains why an object stops, and how it will move on a smooth surface and on a rough one | Gives cases where friction gets in the way and where it helps; suggests how to reduce or increase it |
| **14. Magnets and magnetic materials** (5.4, 5.5) | Thinks all metals are magnetic; doesn't tell the poles apart | Finds magnetic objects; muddles the poles | Finds out by testing which materials are attracted; knows about the two poles and that like poles repel | Predicts how the magnets will behave before testing, then checks; explains why there's a magnet inside a familiar object |
