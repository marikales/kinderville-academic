## The whole-team circle

The only time in the week when the entire primary team can gather: Olga S., Elizaveta, Maria, Margarita, Gaiane, Olga P., Albina, Sam.

It starts at 16:30 — after the children have gone and home time is over. This is the place for what does not fit into pairs and small circles: brainstorming, shared decisions, anything that concerns everyone at once. Every three weeks, one hour.

## The circles do not touch the children's day

No circle sits on reflection or home time: Homeroom, English, Russian and Science run inside the school day, when the children are with other adults, and the whole-team circle starts at 16:30, once home time is finished. Reflection stays daily, with its own curator, in its own place at the end of the day.

## On signal, not on schedule

A small circle about one particular child: the curator plus the two or three subject teachers needed, within a week of the signal. The window is chosen to fit that group.

## How the circles work

There are two types, and they should not be mixed — otherwise the conversation always slides towards the children and the programme never gets discussed.

**Vertical** — one subject across the years: English, Russian, Science. Led by the subject owner; horizon of a module and a year. The unit of discussion is a skill and how it grows from Y2 to Y6: how far we have come, whether we all expect the same, and what the next teacher will receive.

**Horizontal** — all the teachers around a particular group of children. That is Homeroom and the small circles called on signal. Led by the curator; horizon of a week. The agenda is built **class by class**, not as one long block: a subject teacher comes for their own classes and leaves.

Separate from the circles is the curators' daily 8:15 briefing. That one is about today: who has arrived, what we are moving. Homeroom is about the child and the way we do things; unless these are kept apart, the circle turns into a repeat of the briefing within a month.

## What goes in writing instead of a meeting

The science topic table (Russian → English), the Peterson ↔ Cambridge mapping a module ahead, and the shared standard for presenting written work. Anything that passes on a fact rather than works out a decision.
